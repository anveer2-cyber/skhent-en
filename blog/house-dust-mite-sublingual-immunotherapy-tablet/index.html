House Dust Mite Sublingual Immunotherapy Tablets: Common Questions Answered

3 min read Written by Dr. OH JUNGHO, ENT specialist Originally published in Korean: May 15, 2025 Read the original (한국어) ↗

A house dust mite sublingual immunotherapy (SLIT) tablet is a treatment for allergic rhinitis caused by house dust mites. A small amount of the allergen is placed under the tongue every day, training the immune system not to overreact to it.

In other words, repeated exposure to the allergen induces immune tolerance and reduces allergic over-sensitivity. It is a disease-modifying treatment: rather than only easing symptoms, it gradually changes the immune response to house dust mites.

From what age can it be used?

This article describes the 300-IR house dust mite tablet used in Korea; other products may have different age limits and instructions. The product is approved for children aged 5 years and older and for adults. The best time to start depends on symptom burden, confirmed allergy, safety and the ability to follow daily treatment; starting earlier does not guarantee a better result.

Before startingA specialist should confirm that house dust mite allergy is responsible for your symptoms and review asthma control, pregnancy or breastfeeding, mouth problems and all medicines, including beta-blockers, before treatment. It must not be used in severe, uncontrolled or unstable asthma, and other product-specific contraindications also apply.

When does it start to work?

Early in treatment, local reactions such as itching in the mouth or throat discomfort are common, but they are mostly mild and lessen over time. The response differs from person to person and is assessed within the first year.

How is it taken?

  • Follow the instructions for your prescribed product. For the Korean 300-IR tablet described here, place it under the tongue with nothing else in the mouth, allow it to dissolve fully and then swallow. Avoid food, drink and tooth-brushing for five minutes afterwards. The dose is increased in steps over the first few days, as instructed by the doctor.
  • The first dose is taken at the clinic, and you are observed for at least 30 minutes for any reaction. After that, you take it at home every day.

What are the side effects?

Mild, brief mouth itching can occur early in treatment. Ask your prescriber how to manage it; do not use an antihistamine to mask worsening symptoms.

Stop and seek careStop the tablet and call emergency services for breathing difficulty, rapidly increasing tongue or throat swelling, faintness or a severe generalized reaction. Stop treatment and contact your doctor promptly for persistent or severe difficulty swallowing or chest pain. Ask before restarting after mouth sores, dental extraction or oral surgery.

Can I keep taking my usual allergy medicines?

Yes. At first the tablet is used together with antihistamines or nasal sprays. Once the immunotherapy starts to work, you may be able to use those medicines less often or at a lower dose. For more on sprays, see blocked nose and nasal sprays.

How long does treatment last?

If effective and tolerated, treatment is usually planned for several years, commonly 3–5 years. The response should be reviewed during the first year. Evidence after stopping is more limited: randomized studies include about one year of follow-up, while a small observational subgroup reported benefit for up to two years. These findings do not establish ten-year benefit or guarantee how long an individual will improve.

Key points
  • A daily under-the-tongue tablet that gradually changes the immune response to house dust mites
  • The Korean 300-IR tablet is approved from age 5; the first dose is taken under observation at the clinic
  • Local mouth and throat reactions are common early on and usually settle
  • Planned for at least 3 years, usually 3 to 5 years

Keeping indoor humidity in a suitable range also matters for dust mite control; see rhinitis and humidity.
