Can You Get the COVID-19 and Flu Vaccines on the Same Day?

3 min read Written by Dr. OH JUNGHO, ENT specialist Originally published in Korean: September 11, 2024 Read the original (한국어) ↗

As autumn and winter approach, flu season begins in earnest. Because influenza and COVID-19 can circulate together every autumn and winter, many people ask whether they can get both vaccines at one visit. Many people, including high-risk groups with weakened immunity, wonder whether they should get both together.

This article looks at the safety and effectiveness of getting the two vaccines at the same visit.

Why both vaccines matter

COVID-19 and influenza are both respiratory infections that can cause serious complications in people with weaker immunity. Influenza causes many deaths every year, and the risk can be even higher when someone is infected with both viruses at once. Both infections can be prevented by vaccination, and getting the two vaccines together builds protection against both diseases at the same time.

Is it safe?

Several studies have found that giving the COVID-19 and influenza vaccines together is safe. When the two are given together, tiredness, headache and muscle aches may be slightly more common, but they are mostly mild and settle within a few days.

In the 2021 UK ComFluCOV trial, giving selected influenza vaccines with a second dose of a COVID-19 vaccine raised no new safety concerns and preserved antibody responses. The trial did not show that receiving both on the same day prevents more infections or severe illness than receiving them separately. The main advantage is receiving both vaccines without an extra visit.

The CDC's page on getting flu and other vaccines at the same time: Getting vaccines at the same time (CDC).

How well does it work?

Vaccination matters even more for high-risk groups such as older adults and people with chronic illnesses. Getting the two vaccines together produces an immune response similar to getting each one separately, and one visit prepares you for both diseases.

If you are eligible and due for both vaccines, they can usually be given at the same visit at separate injection sites. There is no required waiting interval between these two vaccine types, but the schedule for each vaccine, previous doses, recent illness and current Korean recommendations still need to be checked.

The Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (KDCA) also recommends giving the COVID-19 and influenza vaccines at the same visit. When given on the same day, they are injected at different sites.

What to consider before getting both

The most important thing is to understand your own health. If you have ever had a severe allergic reaction after a vaccine, talk to a doctor before deciding. If you are currently unwell or have a particular medical condition, a consultation is also needed.

Talk to a doctor first if
  • You have had a severe allergic reaction after a previous vaccine
  • You are unwell on the day, or have a medical condition you are unsure about

In summary

For people who are due for both vaccines, same-day vaccination is a convenient option. Each vaccine helps protect against its target disease; protection is not complete or immediate.

If you develop flu-like symptoms, see diagnosing flu on day one and why an early flu test can be negative.
