Phlegm, Postnasal Drip and a Lump in the Throat: Why Nasal Endoscopy Matters

3 min read Written by Dr. OH JUNGHO, ENT specialist Originally published in Korean: March 12, 2020 Read the original (한국어) ↗

"I keep bringing up phlegm." "I can feel mucus running down the back of my throat." "My throat feels sticky." "It feels like something is stuck in my throat." These are common complaints, and the cause is not always where people expect.

Even if it is a little uncomfortable, Dr. OH tends to examine both the nose and the voice box (larynx) with an endoscope. With nasal endoscopy in particular, if the patient can cooperate, the scope is passed all the way through the nose to the very back, the nasopharynx.

A typical presentation

"I've been coughing up phlegm for over a month."

A typical example: there is not much runny nose, but the throat feels rough, there is a slight cough and a feeling of something in the throat, lasting a month or more. Postnasal drainage is one possible cause of throat symptoms, but other causes may need assessment. Your clinician will decide whether nasal endoscopy, laryngoscopy or imaging is appropriate after reviewing your history and examination.

Why look all the way to the back of the nose

In cases like this, the examination doesn't stop at the front of the nose. Even if it is slightly uncomfortable, the endoscope is passed all the way back to the nasopharynx, because sometimes the front of the nose looks fine and the problem is only at the back. The scope is inserted very carefully, and the endoscopes used for the ear and nose are quite thin.

Even without a runny nose, endoscopy can show sticky mucus draining from the back openings of the nose (the choanae) down toward the nasopharynx: postnasal drip.

Checking for sinusitis

Sinusitis is diagnosed from the symptoms and nasal endoscopy. Plain sinus X-rays are not accurate enough and are no longer recommended in current guidelines. A CT scan is used when symptoms persist, when surgery is being considered, or when one side is much worse.

In the example above, the CT scan of the nose and sinuses showed left maxillary sinusitis.

Chronic rhinosinusitis is considered when compatible symptoms persist for at least 12 weeks, usually including nasal blockage or nasal discharge, and inflammation is confirmed on nasal endoscopy or CT. Phlegm or a lump-in-the-throat sensation alone does not establish this diagnosis. A one-month history does not by itself meet the chronic-duration criterion.

Treatment depends on the diagnosis and disease subtype. Saline rinses and intranasal corticosteroids are common treatments for inflammatory chronic rhinosinusitis. Surgery may be discussed when its expected benefit exceeds continued nonsurgical care; a fixed course of medicines is not required for every patient. Unilateral disease, including a possible dental source or fungal ball, needs an individualized plan.

Key pointPhlegm, a sticky throat or a lump-in-the-throat feeling can come from the back of the nose.
Seek emergency careSeek emergency care immediately for new vision changes, swelling around the eye with severe illness, confusion, a severe unusual headache or neck stiffness with sinus symptoms. Do not wait for a routine clinic appointment.
