2026–27 Flu Vaccination in Korea: Start Dates for Children and Older Adults, and When Children Need Two Doses

7 min read Written by Dr. OH JUNGHO, ENT specialist Originally published in Korean: October 20, 2026 Read the original (한국어) ↗

“When can our family get the 2026 flu vaccine?” “I heard the start date was moved earlier this year — does my child need two doses?” This article goes through the 2026–2027 season vaccination schedule by group.

In short, Korea’s 2026–2027 national influenza vaccination programme started earlier than in previous years. As of 20 October 2026, the start dates for children, pregnant women and adults aged 65 and over have all passed. Free vaccination runs until 30 April 2027.

The schedule was brought forward because the flu season began earlier than usual. The Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (KDCA) issued an influenza epidemic advisory on 11 September and, on 16 September, moved the start dates for children and older adults earlier.

Check first: when to be careful before vaccination
  • History of anaphylaxis after a flu vaccine, or severe allergy to a vaccine ingredient: do not proceed with vaccination without a clinician’s assessment. Avoid the vaccine or ingredient responsible for the reaction, and ask whether a suitable alternative is available under Korean guidance.
  • Babies under 6 months old cannot receive the flu vaccine.
  • Moderate or severe illness, such as a high fever: postpone vaccination until you are better.
  • Guillain–Barré syndrome within 6 weeks of a previous flu vaccine: talk to a doctor first.
  • Egg allergy: if the reaction was only hives, most people can be vaccinated, but always tell the doctor before vaccination. If you have had a severe (anaphylactic) reaction to eggs, discuss in advance with a doctor which vaccine to receive and where. An egg allergy is different from a severe allergy to the flu vaccine itself.

1. Who can get the free 2026 flu vaccine, and from when?

GroupStart date
Children aged 6 months to 14 years (born 1 Jan 2012 – 31 Aug 2026)Monday 21 September 2026
Pregnant women (any stage of pregnancy)Monday 21 September 2026
Adults aged 75 and over (born on or before 31 Dec 1951)Tuesday 6 October 2026
Adults aged 70–74 (born 1 Jan 1952 – 31 Dec 1956)Monday 12 October 2026
Adults aged 65–69 (born 1 Jan 1957 – 31 Dec 1961)Thursday 15 October 2026
  • Children: this year, children needing one dose and children needing two doses both started on 21 September. Children needing one dose were originally due to start on 28 September, but this was brought forward by a week. From this season, free vaccination has been extended to age 14.
  • Pregnant women: eligibility is confirmed with a document such as the maternal health record book or a pregnancy confirmation.
  • Adults 65 and over: among those eligible for national vaccination, high-risk people such as those with weakened immunity can be vaccinated from 6 October after talking with the medical staff, regardless of the start date for their age group.

Free vaccination ends on 30 April 2027 for all groups. It is available at designated medical institutions and public health centres nationwide, regardless of your registered address.

2. Why is earlier vaccination advised this year?

According to KDCA data, in the first week of September (week 36) there were 25.3 people with suspected flu per 1,000 outpatients. This exceeded this season’s epidemic threshold (12.9) and was much higher than at the same time last year (6.6).

Cases rose quickly among children aged 7–12 and 1–6. In week 36, adults aged 65 and over accounted for 60% of influenza inpatients reported by sentinel hospitals. These findings helped inform the decision to bring vaccination forward for children and older adults.

It also helps to remember that it takes about 2 weeks after vaccination for protective antibodies to develop. In a year when the epidemic has already started, getting vaccinated even a little earlier is worthwhile.

3. Does my child need two doses?

Children aged 6 months to under 9 years need two doses if any of the following applies:

  • This is their first flu vaccination
  • Their previous vaccination history is unknown
  • They had received only one flu vaccine dose in total by 30 June 2026

The two doses are given at least 4 weeks (about 1 month) apart. Other children receive one dose. The number of doses can differ from child to child, so check it with the doctor at the pre-vaccination check.

4. Can it be given on the same day as the COVID-19 vaccine?

Yes. The KDCA recommends giving the COVID-19 and flu vaccines at the same time. The contraindications and precautions for each vaccine are checked separately.

This season’s free COVID-19 vaccination start dates for adults aged 65 and over are:

  • Aged 70 and over: from Monday 12 October 2026
  • Aged 65–69: from Thursday 15 October 2026
  • People aged 12 and over with weakened immunity, and people aged 12 and over who are inpatients or residents of facilities vulnerable to infection, can be vaccinated from 12 October. Long-term care hospitals and care facilities follow a separate schedule, so check with the facility.

When the two are given together, they are injected at different sites — usually one in each arm, or well apart if in the same arm. Getting both together may cause slightly more reactions such as tiredness or muscle aches, but these are mostly mild and settle within a few days. See also getting the COVID-19 and flu vaccines on the same day.

5. What should I watch for before and after vaccination?

Before vaccination

  • Check whether you can be vaccinated with your local public health centre or on the KDCA vaccination helper website (nip.kdca.go.kr) first. The schedule changed this year, so preparation may differ between institutions.
  • Bring identification. Children are confirmed with a resident registration record or health insurance card, and pregnant women with the maternal health record book or similar.
  • On the pre-vaccination questionnaire, record any fever, allergies and reactions to previous vaccines.

After vaccination

  • Stay at the vaccination site for about 20–30 minutes to check for any adverse reaction before going home.
  • Redness, swelling or pain at the injection site can occur in 15–20% of people and usually improves within 1–2 days.
  • Fever, muscle aches, headache and weakness can occur in fewer than 1% and usually last 1–2 days.
  • These figures are the general figures the KDCA gives for an inactivated flu vaccine given on its own; they can differ by age and vaccine type.
Seek emergency careIf a severe allergic reaction is suspected after vaccination — for example breathlessness or swelling of the throat, face or lips — call 119 immediately or get assessed at an emergency department. If you are still at the vaccination site, tell the medical staff straight away. With severe symptoms, do not drive yourself or wait for a routine clinic appointment.

If symptoms get worse or last a long time, see a doctor. Adverse reactions can be reported through a public health centre or medical institution.

6. Can I still catch flu after being vaccinated?

Yes. The protection from the flu vaccine is not 100%. You may be exposed to a flu virus not included in the vaccine, or be infected before antibodies develop (about 2 weeks after vaccination). The aim of vaccination is to reduce the risk of hospital admission and severe illness.

So, along with vaccination, keep up basic measures: hand washing, covering coughs, wearing a mask in crowded places and ventilating indoor spaces. If you are at high risk — for example aged 65 or over, or with weakened immunity — it is a good idea to see a doctor early if you develop symptoms such as fever and cough. If you test negative very early in the illness, see why an early flu test can be negative.

Summary: 2026 flu vaccination schedule
  • Children (6 months to 14 years) and pregnant women: free vaccination from 21 September
  • Aged 75+ from 6 October, 70–74 from 12 October, 65–69 from 15 October
  • Free vaccination until 30 April 2027, regardless of registered address
  • Children from 6 months to under 9 years who are being vaccinated for the first time, whose history is unknown, or who had only one dose by 30 June 2026: two doses at least 4 weeks apart
  • Can be given on the same day as the COVID-19 vaccine (KDCA recommendation)
  • High-risk adults aged 65+: can be vaccinated from 6 October after consultation, regardless of age group
  • Severe allergic symptoms: call 119 or go to an emergency department immediately
  • Stay 20–30 minutes at the vaccination site after the injection

Schedules can change according to KDCA announcements, so check the latest information on the vaccination helper website before vaccination.

Official sources: KDCA, 2026–2027 National Influenza Vaccination Programme; KDCA schedule update, 16 September 2026; KDCA influenza surveillance update, 11 September 2026; KDCA COVID-19 vaccination programme; KDCA vaccine adverse reactions (influenza).
